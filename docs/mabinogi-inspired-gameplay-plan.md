# Mabinogi-inspired gameplay implementation plan

Build a playable slice of Rebirth Dungeon around one procedurally generated floor. The player should be able to read an enemy's intent, win a melee encounter, collect and equip loot, train a skill, and complete a quest that reveals a clue about the dungeon.

## What to adapt from Mabinogi

- **Opening and travel.** The archived opening guides a new character from an arrival space to a hometown chief, then introduces equipment and combat. Rebirth Dungeon starts in the Waking Vault with Ilex and sends the player through Lantern Rest and a hostile passage to Spindle Hollow. Dungeon Towns house the elder, smith, provisioner, and healer. Players visit and register Moon Gates in person before using them for fast travel. [Nexon beginner guide](https://nxcache.nexon.net/mabinogi/microsite/BeginnerGuide/), [classic beginner quests](https://wiki.mabinogiworld.com/view/Beginner_Quests_(Human_Uladh)), [Moon Gate](https://wiki.mabinogiworld.com/view/Moon_Gate)
- **Combat.** Nexon's basic combat guide describes active, real-time combat with three foundational melee interactions. Attack beats Smash, Defense stops Attack, and Smash breaks Defense. Enemy preparation cues and behavior patterns help players predict what to use. The Defense guidebook also describes a successful guard as reducing incoming damage. Rebirth Dungeon adapts those counters and cues to discrete turns and calls the guard action **Defend**. [Nexon basic combat guide](https://mabinogi.nexon.co.jp/guide/battle.asp), [Nexon Defense Guidebook](https://mabibook.nexon.net/usa/guidebook_defence.html)
- **Skills and talents.** Mabinogi lets a character learn skills across disciplines. Using skills in qualifying ways trains them, and eligible ranks can be purchased with Ability Points (AP). A chosen talent improves training for related skills. Adapt this as individual skill ranks and talent bonuses, with original skill families and progression rates. [Nexon skill system guide](https://mabinogi.nexon.co.jp/guide/skill.asp)
- **Inventory.** Mabinogi uses a grid, item footprints, equipment slots, and bags that occupy space while providing their own storage grids. Adapt those spatial tradeoffs to loot and equipment decisions between encounters, with keyboard controls. [Nexon bag guide](https://mabinogi.nexon.com/page/archive/guide_view.asp?id=4888919), [Nexon inventory update](https://mabinogi.nexon.com/page/news/notice_view.asp?id=4889931)

## First combat rules

This table defines the proposed Rebirth Dungeon prototype for two adjacent combatants. Damage refers to the selected action's damage. Bypassing Defend skips the stance's damage reduction. Armor remains a separate modifier when equipment is added.

| Player action | Enemy Normal Attack | Enemy Defend | Enemy Smash |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Normal Attack** | Both deal normal damage. | Player deals reduced normal damage. | Player deals normal damage and interrupts the enemy's Smash. |
| **Defend** | Enemy deals reduced normal damage. | Neither takes damage. | Enemy breaks the player's guard and deals Smash damage. |
| **Smash** | Enemy deals normal damage and interrupts the player's Smash. | Player breaks the enemy's guard and deals Smash damage. | Both deal Smash damage. |

1. The enemy commits an action and the UI shows a stance or preparation cue. The enemy keeps its commitment while the player inspects the map or menus.
2. The player confirms an available action against the enemy. Canceling selection or choosing an invalid action leaves the turn unchanged.
3. Resolve the pair from the same starting state. Apply any damage to both combatants before checking defeat, so equal-action exchanges are independent of evaluation order. A double knockout ends the run as a defeat for the prototype.
4. Log the actions, the reason for the outcome, and the damage. End the encounter or advance the turn once and prepare the next enemy intent.

Use 4 Normal Attack damage, 7 Smash damage, and 50% Defend mitigation as initial test values. Store these values separately from the matchup table. The first enemy should clearly demonstrate all three cues through a short, repeatable pattern. Use behavior that varies for later enemies once the cues are readable.

## Build order

1. **Build a deterministic melee sandbox.** Make a GDScript resolver that takes the committed actions and combat state and returns damage and outcome events. Keep it independent of scene nodes, UI, and enemy AI. Wire it into a small test scene with one enemy and keyboard actions. Check all nine matchups, both role reversals for each counter, Defend's damage reduction, equal-action exchanges, invalid input, and double knockouts. The same inputs must produce the same outcome and consume exactly one turn when valid.
2. **Connect combat to dungeon exploration.** Use Gaea to build a small seeded floor with reachable rooms and corridors. Use GECS for actor state such as grid position, health, and equipment. The world turn controller owns movement and encounter timing. Beehave selects a legal enemy intent from the starting turn state before player commitment, then the combat resolver adjudicates it. Start with one active melee opponent at a time. Keep the grid visible and add a compact Departure Mono action panel, intent cue, and message log. Playtest a full encounter through Beckett and confirm that examining menus neither advances the dungeon nor changes enemy intent.
3. **Add item and equipment decisions.** Give each item definition a stable ID, width, height, stack limit, and optional equipment slot. Use one base inventory grid and one bag type that occupies a footprint and opens its own grid. Implement pickup, placement, stacking, equip, unequip, and drop with keyboard controls. Validate bounds, overlap, and stack or slot compatibility before committing a move. Keep the source item in place on failed transfers. Check that full storage and occupied equipment slots never lose or duplicate items, and that equipping a weapon changes damage without changing the counter rules.
4. **Add skill ranks and talents.** Give skill definitions qualifying events, rank thresholds, and AP costs. Start with Smash, Defend, and one exploration or salvage skill. For example, a guard break trains Smash and a successful damage reduction trains Defend. Award AP through the starter quest and let players spend it after meeting the training threshold. Add two talent specializations with configurable bonuses to their associated training. Show rank, progress, and next-rank cost in the skills menu. Verify that repeated UI operations cannot award training, rank-up spending is applied once, and skills outside the selected talent remain trainable.
5. **Close the loop with a quest and save point.** Use Dialogue Manager for one original branching NPC conversation that sends the player into the dungeon and rewards a returned object with AP and a lore clue. Use Source Code Pro for dialogue and journal text. Save the run at a resolved turn boundary with the floor seed, actor state, item placements, skill progress, talent, and quest state. Verify the complete loop through Beckett, including reload and runtime logs. After this slice works, add more combat and noncombat skill families, talents, and enemy patterns in small batches.
